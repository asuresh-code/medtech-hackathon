Essentials: what Freya must do
Short, regular check-ins. Freya prompts her every few days, at times it learns from when she usually responds. The EPDS questions are split across these sessions so each one is only a few questions, which keeps the mental load low. The full set is completed within each 7-day window, so the weekly total stays meaningful.
Treat silence as a signal. Missed check-ins and missed classes, combined with a rise from her own baseline, trigger a “quiet drift” flag. Nothing else in the current system does this.
Compare her to herself. Flag a change from her own baseline, not just a score over 13. That’s how we catch the women below the specialist threshold.
Every flag has a named owner. Each level routes to a specific person (health visitor, GP or perinatal team) with a stated response time. A flag nobody reads is worse than no flag.
A one-page handover. Whoever picks her up gets her trend, attendance, scores and the reason the flag fired, so they can act in minutes.
An immediate urgent route. Any positive answer to EPDS question 10 is flagged as top priority straight away. She immediately sees crisis contacts (999 if in immediate danger, Samaritans 116 123, local crisis line), and the named professional is alerted to contact her the same day.
No admin for her. Setup and sign-in happen at a first in-person session, so she never has to deal with it herself.
A reason to come back. A warm “your week” message after each check-in, plus the studio community.
Trust from day one. At that first session, a clinician explains in plain words who sees her answers, where the data goes and why. A person explaining it is more reassuring than a consent screen in the app.
The do-nots
Safety
Don’t let the AI score, triage or reassure. Rules decide every flag. The AI only writes the summary, and the raw scores always sit beside it.
Don’t ask about self-harm without a guaranteed response. If we can’t name who reviews urgent flags and how fast, we don’t ship.
Don’t let the urgent question be skipped or answered by accident. It needs a deliberate answer.
Her experience
Don’t show her scores, thresholds or verdicts. That includes reassuring ones like “You’re doing fine, nothing to worry about!”, which can stop her asking for help. Scores, thresholds and priority sorting belong on the clinician’s side only.
Don’t use streaks, guilt or “you missed…” messages. Using Freya should feel stress-free.
Don’t over-notify. Send one prompt per check-in at her usual time, with at most one gentle reminder.
Do include a night mode. Use soft, warm cream tones and low blue light, since she’ll often use it before bed or after settling the baby.
